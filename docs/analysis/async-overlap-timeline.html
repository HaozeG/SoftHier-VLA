SmolVLA VLM–Expert Overlap on H1

How much two speedups from the SJTU paper would shorten one SmolVLA call: the overlap of the VLM with the first flow steps, and DP-Cache. Inputs are the stage-1 kernel sums after the expert GEMMs moved to the full mesh (commit dcd221d, 28.99 ms per call). Methods from Zhou et al. (SJTU), arXiv 2604.24447, §5.2.2 and §5.3. Numbers come from docs/analysis/async_overlap_estimate.py.

How to read this page

Today one SmolVLA call runs in two strict phases on all 64 clusters: the VLM (vision encoder, then prefill) for 17.86 ms, then 10 flow steps of the action expert for 11.13 ms. The page compares two ways from the SJTU paper to shorten that call, alone and together.

Not to scale. Each call starts from a new observation (obs t); the robot is drawn stopped while the model runs, as in the paper's Fig. 9. Overlap: the first 5 flow steps run during the VLM, on the VLM output (KV cache) kept from the previous call, t−1; once the VLM finishes, the last 5 steps use the fresh KV of call t. DP-Cache: some flow steps reuse an earlier step's result instead of being computed. The figures below draw the same calls to scale.

DP-CacheSkip some flow steps by reusing the result of an earlier step. The mapping does not change; the expert phase just gets shorter.
Overlap V-AEFusion in the paperStart the first k = 5 flow steps before the VLM finishes, on the previous call's VLM output ("stale" steps). After the VLM finishes, run the remaining 5 steps on the fresh output. The question is how the mesh runs the VLM and the stale steps at the same time:
Overlap, idealAssumes both run at full speed with no interference. A cluster can only run one kernel at a time, so this is an upper bound, not a real mapping.
Overlap, 8- or 16-cluster splitGive 8 or 16 clusters to the stale steps and the rest to the VLM. Both slow down; the measured sub-mesh kernel times and a linear VLM slowdown set how much.

After the VLM finishes, every variant runs the fresh steps on the full mesh, so the variants differ only in the overlap phase.

DP-Cache steps, drawn like the paper's Fig. 7

Assumed pattern: steps 4 and 5 reuse step 3, steps 7 and 8 reuse step 6 (4 of 10 reused). The paper fixes its stable segment only for a 100-step diffusion (steps 20 to 80, S = 4); 4 of 10 is chosen so the saved share of expert time (40 %) matches the paper's π0 result (102 → 79 ms, about 38 % of expert time). Summary

One-tick rate = the highest control rate at which a call still finishes within one control period (1 / latency). Overlap and DP-Cache do not multiply: 1.18 × 1.235 would be 1.46×, the model gives 1.36×, because the stale steps are already hidden behind the VLM and caching there saves nothing. Design rule: place reused steps in the fresh tail.

What the controller sees

20 Hz control (50 ms period, the test environment's rate; LIBERO runs at 20 Hz, VLABench at 10 Hz), 50-action chunks, a new call every 25 ticks (an example). Stop-and-go (the paper's Fig. 9 view): the robot pauses for one call; at 20 Hz every scenario fits in one tick, so the pause shrinks from 29.0 to 21.3 ms in a 1,250 ms cycle (2.3 % → 1.7 % of the time). In simulation the world also pauses during inference, so this cost does not appear in success rates. Asynchronous execution (RTC, SmolVLA async): the call hides under the queue; latency sets observation staleness (29.0 → 21.3 ms, 1 tick at 10, 20 and 30 Hz, 2 ticks at 50 Hz). In both modes the stale steps read a prefix cache that is one replan interval old (25 ticks = 1,250 ms here), not one latency old; the paper's coherence measurement (KV cosine 97.99 % / 96.91 %) is over that same interval, a chunk's execution.

Spatial mapping per phase

8 × 8 clusters of H1. Drawn with cluster (0, 0) at the bottom left. The stage-1 kernels place their groups starting at cluster (0, 0) and have no origin offset, so the stale-step group takes the origin rows and the VLM would need kernels that can start elsewhere. Which HBM edge the origin row touches is not checked here. After dcd221d the fresh phase is the same in every scenario (expert on the full mesh); the variants differ only in the overlap phase.

Assumptions and what to measure next

  • VLM on 56 or 48 clusters is scaled linearly (64/56, 64/48). This decides the split variants: a split pays off only while the VLM slows by less than the hidden expert time, 5 steps × 1.10 = 5.52 ms, i.e. at most 1.31× on 17.86 ms. At c = 16 the linear slowdown (1.33×) already loses. Measure VLM kernels on 56 and 48 clusters first.
  • Expert stale step on c clusters is confined to one fixed footprint at the mesh origin: one row of 8 (c = 8) or two rows of 8 (c = 16). GEMMs: the fastest measured setting whose active groups lie inside that footprint (2.18 ms per step on the row of 8, 1.35 ms on two rows, 0.73 ms on the full mesh). Attention: the measured setting spreads the 5 KV heads over 5 groups on 40 clusters, so no sub-mesh time exists; inside the footprint fewer groups fit and the heads run in rounds (0.57 ms per step on the row of 8, 0.34 ms on two rows, 0.20 ms on the full mesh; estimate). Row operators: unchanged to 64/c slower (upper bound used). On the row of 8 a stale step takes 2.93 to 4.14 ms; at the upper end five stale steps (20.7 ms) outlast the VLM on 56 clusters (20.42 ms), so the expert, not the VLM, sets the overlap time.
  • The VLM on the remaining clusters is likely slower than linear: power-of-two groups tile only part of an 8 × 7 or 8 × 6 region (4 × 4 groups cover 8 × 4 = 32 clusters, 2 × 2 groups 8 × 6 = 48). If the VLM on 8 × 7 behaves like 48 clusters (1.33×), the 8-cluster split also loses.
  • Not modelled: NoC and HBM contention between the two groups; the reconfiguration from a c-cluster group to the full mesh at the KV switch; two kernels sharing a cluster (a cluster runs one kernel at a time, so the ideal overlap has no physical mapping on SoftHier and is a bound).
  • Accuracy: no source measures the combination. SJTU's stale-step limit (≤ 5 of 10, Table 7) is for π0 on a real dual-arm robot. In SmolVLA the robot state sits in the VLM prefix and the self-attention layers also read the prefix cache, so stale steps carry stale state as well as stale images.
  • DP-Cache's 4-of-10 pattern is a calibration to π0, not a SmolVLA measurement; the cached data (one 50 × 32 velocity per reused step) is negligible in memory.
